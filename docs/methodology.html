What this tool does

A few times a day it pulls the most active markets from Polymarket and Kalshi, figures out which markets on each platform are betting on the same real-world event, and flags where the two platforms disagree on the odds by more than 5% after fees.

Informational only. The tool never places a trade and never touches money. A flagged signal is an observation, not a recommendation — acting on one is a separate, manual decision you make yourself. This is not financial advice.

How to read the scanner

  1. The scatter is the picture. Each point is one matched pair: its Polymarket probability on the x-axis, its Kalshi probability (inversion-adjusted) on the y-axis. The dashed diagonal is perfect agreement. Distance from the diagonal is the edge — points hugging the line mean the platforms agree; points far from it mean they disagree.
  2. Color encodes the gap.
    on the line — agreement far off — large gap resolution mismatch warning
  3. Click anything to inspect it. Click a point or a table row to open the detail card: both prices (mid and bid/ask), gross and net spread, the dollar edge at a $100 position, volume on each side, confidence, end date, links to both markets, and a small spread-over-time sparkline.
  4. Three ranked tables. Best net ROI sorts by net spread. Most likely to converge sorts by recent spread velocity for now (it switches to the convergence model once enough history exists). Highest confidence sorts by match confidence.
  5. Filters narrow the view. Category, minimum net spread, minimum liquidity, minimum confidence, and a toggle to hide resolution mismatches. A sensible starting point: net spread ≥ 5%, confidence ≥ 0.80, mismatches hidden.

How a signal is computed

1 · Ingestion

Public market data from both platforms is normalized into one schema — same fields, same units — so a Polymarket market and a Kalshi market can be compared directly.

2 · Matching

Deciding that two differently-worded questions are the same event is the hard part and the main place a tool like this goes wrong. Matching runs in stages: sentence embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) find candidate pairs by meaning, then a Claude alignment agent confirms each pair, aligns the outcomes, and returns a confidence score. We'd rather show 20 matches you can trust than 100 with junk mixed in, so low-confidence matches are flagged separately.

Inversion is handled explicitly. If Polymarket-Yes corresponds to Kalshi-No, the comparison uses 1 − kalshi so the diagonal still means agreement. Inverted pairs are badged as such.

3 · Spread

Two prices do two different jobs:

  • Midpoint drives the scatter — a clean "do they agree?" picture.
  • Executable bid/ask drives the actionable net spread in the tables, because real arbitrage means paying to cross the spread on both legs.
gross_spread = |pm_prob − kalshi_aligned|
net_spread   = gross_spread − pm_fee − kalshi_fee

4 · Fees

A raw 6% gap can vanish entirely after fees — and both platforms charge the most exactly where gaps look most tempting (near 50/50 odds). Fees peak at p = 0.5 and taper toward the extremes. Polymarket fees scale by market category (crypto is the priciest, geopolitics is free); Kalshi follows a probability curve peaking near ~1.75%. We assume the worst realistic case — taker fees on both legs — so the tool never overstates an opportunity.

The fee curves are validated approximations, not guarantees. Treat the net edge as a strong estimate. Limit orders ("maker") pay zero fees on both platforms, so the real edge can be better than shown.

5 · Opportunity threshold

A pair is flagged as an opportunity when net_spread ≥ 5%. The detail card illustrates the dollar edge at a standard $100 position: est_edge_usd = net_spread × 100. A resolution-mismatch warning attaches when the two markets may not resolve on identical criteria.

How signals are scored over time

A signal is only meaningful if you can check whether it was real. When a matched market actually settles, the outcome is recorded — including whether both legs resolved to the same real-world result, the strongest possible proof the match was genuine. The Track record page reports, with sample sizes:

  • Hit rate — share of flagged signals whose spread converged (below a locked epsilon of 0.02) before resolution.
  • Days to converge — median and full distribution.
  • Captured vs. realized — flagged spread vs. how much actually closed.
  • Match quality — share of resolved pairs that resolved consistently.
Honesty principles, baked in: failed and expired signals are never quietly dropped — they count against the record. Metric definitions are locked before they are measured, scoring never peeks at the resolution to pick the flag point, and small samples are labeled as small, not dressed up as conclusions.

How it runs

The system is split so it can run itself for free:

  • GitHub Actions is the engine — a scheduled job that does all fetching, matching, ML, database writes, and email. Secrets live only here.
  • This site is the window — it just displays a static data.json the engine produces. No secrets, no API calls from your browser.
  • Postgres is the memory — it stores the history the scoring and ML need.

Because the browser only reads a static file, there's nothing here to attack and nothing to leak.

Informational only — computed signals net of conservative taker fees, not financial advice. Net spread = gross gap − fees on both legs. Acting on a signal is a separate manual decision.
